Problem 3 - Fourier-Domain Edge Detection and Segmentation

Image transform: F(u,v) = F{f(x,y)}

Smooth image regions change slowly and are dominated by low spatial frequencies. Object boundaries contain rapid intensity changes and therefore contribute strongly to higher spatial frequencies.

Frequency filtering: G(u,v) = H(u,v)F(u,v)

Return to the image domain: g(x,y) = F-1{G(u,v)}

A high-pass filter suppresses slowly varying components while retaining rapid intensity transitions. After the inverse transform, strong responses occur near boundaries and can be thresholded to form an edge map.

Derivative relation:

F{∂f/∂x} = j2πuF(u,v),    F{∂f/∂y} = j2πvF(u,v)

Differentiation in the image domain therefore corresponds to multiplication by frequency in the Fourier domain. Large high-frequency responses indicate rapid spatial change, which is the mathematical basis for frequency-domain edge detection.

Region segmentation:

A low-pass filter can first suppress noise and small texture. A high-pass or band-pass response can then emphasize region boundaries. After inverse transformation, thresholding and connected-component analysis can convert the filtered response into segmented regions.
